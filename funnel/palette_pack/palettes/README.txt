TEXEL MATERIAL PALETTES
=======================

24 colour ramps, 8 steps each, 216 colours in total.
GIMP palette format (.gpl), which Texel, Aseprite, Krita and GIMP all read.

Public domain (CC0). Use them for anything, commercial or not, no credit needed.


WHAT IS IN HERE
---------------

Stone:
  Granite, Marble, Slate, Redbrick, Sandstone, Loam

Nature:
  Moss, Oakwood, Pine, Seafoam, Deepwater, Orchid

Metal:
  Verdigris, Copper, Gold, Steel, Bronze, Rust

Effect:
  Ember, Torchlight, Arcane, Venom, Frostfire, Bloodmoon

_all-midtones.gpl is a 24-colour sheet holding one mid step from each
ramp, for blocking out a scene before you commit to the full ramps.

reference-sheet.png shows all of them with their end hex values.


HOW THE RAMPS WERE MADE
-----------------------

Every ramp is generated by make_ramp() from Texel, a pixel-art add-on for
Blender. It does two things a plain darken-to-black ramp does not:

  - the hue ROTATES across the ramp instead of staying fixed. Measured
    across these 24 palettes the rotation runs +16.4 to +32.9 degrees,
    darkest step to lightest.
  - saturation peaks in the MIDTONES rather than climbing with brightness.

Value span per ramp runs 0.42 to 0.66 in HSV. Every ramp is checked
to be strictly increasing dark-to-light, to hold no duplicate entries, and
to neither crush its darkest step to black nor clip its lightest to white.

No two palettes' midtones sit closer than 12 dE76, and the closest pair in
the set is 14.0 dE - so the 24 are 24 distinct colours rather than a
few repeated under different names. The first draft failed that check seven
times over and was rebuilt around hue coverage instead of around names.


WHAT THIS PACK DOES NOT CLAIM
-----------------------------

It would be easy to say these are 'tuned for 3D', and that a palette for lit
3D needs more midtones than a 2D one. That was the plan. It is not claimed
here, because it was tested twice and neither test supported it:

  1. Pooling every ramp entry across five light levels in linear space and
     measuring CIELAB distance between neighbouring lit swatches: a 4-step
     and a 7-step ramp over the same value range gave an identical worst
     gap, and the number of visible jumps went UP with more steps.
  2. The opposite idea - that a lit renderer supplies the contrast, so a
     3D palette wants a narrower albedo span - showed no discrimination
     either: every span from 0.20 to 0.80 kept 100% of its steps distinct.

So: these are good, well-separated material ramps. Whether they suit lit 3D
better than any other good palette is not something we measured, so it is
not something we will tell you.


LOADING THEM
------------

  Texel      Palette panel > Load, pick the .gpl
  Aseprite   Palette menu > Load Palette
  Krita      Settings > Dockers > Palettes, import
  GIMP       Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Palettes > Import

A .gpl is a plain text file. Open one in any editor if you want the hexes.
