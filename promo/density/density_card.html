Texel · detect & apply density

Texel measures how big a texel really is:
8.4× spread across one mesh, then 1.0×.

A 3.2 m wall and a 0.38 m crate share one 32 px texture on Blender's default cube UVs, and those UVs do not change with a cube's size. So the texels land 8.4× bigger on the wall — exactly the size ratio.
mesh 18 faces
texture 32×32 px
target 10.52 px/unit
It corrects in both directions — the wall gains detail, the crate's pixels grow. Every figure is printed by texel.density_detect inside Blender. Even the after-figure is a second measurement of the finished mesh, not the 10.52 it was asked for.
