Texel paints pixel art straight onto your models inside Blender — and animates it. It fixes the two things that go wrong when 2D pixel art meets a mesh: strokes that come out as staircases, and pixels that change size from one face to the next.

What it does:

  • Pixel-perfect strokes. A freehand drag lands clean, with no doubled corners to pick out by hand.
  • Texel density, measured. Reports px/unit across a mesh, rescales UVs to a target, and snaps UVs to texel corners.
  • Tiles that actually tile. Wraps a texture against itself and scores the seam before you commit it.
  • Indexed colour. A 256-entry palette. Swap it and every surface recolours instantly and exactly.
  • Cel animation. Frames and tracks, onion skin, bound to Blender’s timeline. Export a sheet plus JSON, or a GIF.
  • 94 operators, every one with a button and a keyboard shortcut.
