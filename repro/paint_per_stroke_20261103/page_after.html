Texel - Pixel Art Painting for Blender

A downloadable tool

Buy Now$9.95 USD or more

Texel paints pixel art straight onto your models inside Blender — and animates it. It fixes the two things that go wrong when 2D pixel art meets a mesh: strokes that come out as staircases, and pixels that change size from one face to the next.

What it does:

  • Pixel-perfect strokes. A freehand drag lands clean, with no doubled corners to pick out by hand.
  • Texel density, measured. Reports px/unit across a mesh, rescales UVs to a target, and snaps UVs to texel corners.
  • Tiles that actually tile. Wraps a texture against itself and scores the seam before you commit it.
  • Indexed colour. A 256-entry palette. Swap it and every surface recolours instantly and exactly.
  • Selection transforms. Flip, rotate and scale a selection by nearest neighbour — nothing is interpolated between two palette indices, and the mask turns with the art, so a wand selection survives a rotation as its own shape. New in v0.2.0.
  • Radial symmetry. One stroke paints up to sixteen copies around the canvas centre, and it stacks with the mirrors: radial 4 plus Mirror X paints eight. New in v0.2.1.
  • Dither as a brush mode. Paint through a 50%, 25% or 75% mask, or a 4x4 Bayer map. The pattern is fixed to the canvas, so overlapping strokes line up. New in v0.2.2.
  • Stamps. Select a patch of texels, press Stamp From Selection, and the brush paints that patch in its own colours. Transparent texels leave the canvas alone. New in v0.2.4.
  • Tablet pressure. The pen icon beside Size makes pen pressure scale the Pencil and Eraser from 1 texel up to Size. Off by default, and a mouse always presses fully, so with a mouse nothing changes. Tested with simulated pressure values, not yet on a physical tablet. New in v0.2.6.
  • Cel animation. Frames and tracks, onion skin, bound to Blender’s timeline. Export a sheet plus JSON, or a GIF.
  • 95 operators, every one with a button in the sidebar, and nine on keys you can rebind in Blender’s own keymap editor.

Try it free first. Texel Lite is the free demo file under the buy button. It paints, layers, palettes, selections, zones and texel density the same way the full add-on does. It does not include cel animation, sprite sheet, GIF and JSON export, trim, colour count, reference images or Showcase renders.

Getting started. Both zips, Lite and full, include START-HERE.html (an offline guide that opens in your browser) and a full README.md. The first five minutes:

  1. Keep the zip zipped. In Blender: Edit → Preferences → Add-ons → the ▾ menu top-right → Install from Disk… and pick the zip. On Blender 4.2–4.4 the button is Install…
  2. In a 3D Viewport, press N to open the sidebar and click the Texel tab on its right-hand edge. That tab is the whole add-on.
  3. Texel → Setup → Add 1m Cube. One button makes the cube, an image, a material and a UV map, with the texture filter set so texels stay square.
  4. Pick a colour, choose the Pencil, and draw on the cube.

Not you being dumb, that's how 0.2.6 works, and it's the wrong design. Each press of Texel Paint, or the D key with the cursor over the canvas (quicker than the button), starts one stroke: it follows your cursor and ends when you click or let go of the left mouse button, and the next stroke needs another press. Nothing is bound to the mouse button alone because left-click is Blender's select. A proper paint mode, where you switch painting on once and draw until you press Esc, is queued as the next fix after 0.2.7. Thanks for sticking with it, and for the kind words about the demo.
